Nobody shortlists forty people because interviewing is hard. They shortlist because finding forty mutually convenient half-hours is impossible. Take the calendar out and the constraint disappears.

  • No two calendars have to agree. Which is what makes interviewing every applicant a practical option rather than an aspiration.

What the Recorded round does

Takes and think time — what the candidate controls+
Both are yours to set, per question. One take tests composure and gives you something close to a live answer. Three takes tests thinking, and tells you what somebody produces when they can gather themselves. Think time works the same way: thirty seconds is a pressure test, two minutes is a reasoning test. Deciding this deliberately is most of what separates a useful recorded round from a bad one.
The check before the round+
Before recording anything, the candidate runs a short check on camera, microphone and connection, and identity and document collection is handled in chat first. Nobody loses a round to a broken headset, and you only review people who were validated and ready. It all happens in the browser, with nothing to install.
Question sets that adapt+
Reusable question sets grouped into sections, with multiple-choice items mixed in where a knowledge check makes sense, and an interview that adapts based on what has been answered. Question generation runs in the background, so a newly opened role does not wait on somebody writing a bank from scratch.
Scoring, with the moment attached+
Answers are scored against your rubric by a model tier reserved for scoring, validated against a fixed schema rather than accepted as free text. Each score links back to the transcript excerpt and the moment of video it came from — which is what lets a panel settle a disagreement by watching rather than arguing.
Body language and sentiment+
Posture, eye contact, gestures and composure are turned into structured, reviewable signal. It is one input among several. A candidate recording at eleven at night in a shared flat is not short of composure, and the page would be lying if it implied otherwise.
Parallel review, pipelines and leaderboards+
The real second bottleneck in hiring is getting three busy people into the same room. Here they each watch the same round whenever they can, rating against weighted scorecards, with the results collected in one pipeline rather than three inboxes.

Frequently asked questions

Do candidates need to install an app to record a video interview?+
No. The round runs in the browser with nothing to install. Before recording, each candidate runs a short check on their camera, microphone and connection, and identity and document collection is handled in chat first, so you only review people who were validated and ready.
Can candidates re-record their answers, and can I control how long they get to think?+
Yes. Both the number of takes and the think time are set by you, per question. One take tests composure and gives you something close to a live answer; three takes tests thinking and shows what somebody produces when they can gather themselves. Think time works the same way: thirty seconds is a pressure test, two minutes is a reasoning test.
How are recorded interview answers scored, and can my panel check the AI's reasoning?+
Answers are scored against your own rubric by a model tier reserved for scoring, with output validated against a fixed schema rather than accepted as free text. Every score links back to the transcript excerpt and the moment of video it came from, so a panel that disagrees can settle it by watching the clip.
How accurate is the AI scoring, and can body-language analysis alone screen a candidate out?+
TalbotIQ publishes no accuracy figure for the scoring. Body language and sentiment are treated as one input among several, and TalbotIQ states that this signal should never on its own be the reason somebody is passed over.
When is a recorded video interview the wrong format to use?+
It is a screening instrument, not a conversation: the candidate cannot ask you anything, which works against you on a senior or competitive hire, and that is what the 2-Way round is for. It also needs a camera, a microphone and somewhere passable to sit, so the Voice and Chat rounds exist for candidates who have none of those.
Where are candidate video recordings stored, and are they kept forever?+
Recordings sit in private storage and are reached only through short-lived signed URLs. Lifecycle rules expire the media on a schedule rather than keeping it indefinitely; the schedule itself is not stated on this page.
