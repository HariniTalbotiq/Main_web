A recording can be
re-watched. A phone screen cannot.

The first-round phone screen exists in one person’s memory and a few lines of notes. A recorded round is the same interview for everyone, reviewable by anyone on the panel, with the score attached to the moment that produced it.

  • The candidate answers on their time; you review on yours. Which is what makes interviewing every applicant possible instead of interviewing the ones you had slots for.
  • Every score links to its moment. A rating is a claim, and the claim points at the seconds of video that support it — so a panel disagreement is settled by watching rather than arguing.
  • Non-verbal signal, made structured. Posture, eye contact, gestures and composure are read and reported as reviewable intelligence rather than an interviewer’s gut feeling written down afterwards.
  • Hiring managers rate the round itself — not a recruiter’s summary of it. Weighted scorecards mean five people can assess the same candidate against the same criteria.
  • Two modes for the harder rounds. An avatar assessor runs the identical script for every candidate, and a two-way live round puts your own team in the conversation — both scored the same way as the async round.

What the Video Interview does

The check that happens before the round+
Before a candidate records anything, they run a short check on camera, microphone and connection — and identity and document collection is handled in chat first. Two consequences: nobody loses a round to a broken headset, and you only ever review people who were validated and ready. The candidate does all of it in a browser, with nothing to install.
Question sets that adapt as they go+
Reusable question sets grouped into sections, with multiple-choice items mixed in where a knowledge check makes sense, and an interview that adapts based on what has been answered. Question generation runs in the background, so a newly opened role does not wait on somebody writing a bank of questions from scratch.
Scoring, and the evidence underneath it+
Every answer is scored against your rubric by a model tier reserved for scoring, and validated against a fixed schema rather than accepted as free text. Each score links back to the transcript excerpt and the moment of video it came from — so a rating can always be traced to something the candidate actually said, and a panel can go and watch it.
Body language and sentiment, read carefully+
Emotional tone and confidence — posture, eye contact, gestures, composure — are turned into structured, reviewable signal. This is the analysis only a video round can produce, and it is presented as one input among several rather than a verdict. A human still makes every call.
Avatar and two-way live+
An avatar assessor is a composed, bias-free interviewer that runs the identical script for every candidate, which removes the variance between a first interview and a fortieth. A two-way live round puts your own team in a real conversation. Both are scored against the same rubric as the async round, so a live score and a recorded score sit on the same axis.
Pipelines, leaderboards and centralised evaluation+
Candidates move through pipelines with weighted scorecards and leaderboards, so evaluation lives in one place instead of five inboxes. Hiring managers rate the same recorded round rather than a summary of it, and transcripts and analytics are retained for reporting on the process itself.

A recording of somebody’s
career

Video is the most sensitive thing you will hold about a person who does not yet work for you. Three constraints, all structural.

A human makes the call

It ranks and evidences. It does not reject anyone. The hire, the pass and the pipeline stage are decisions a named person takes, on the record.

One AI gateway, no loose ends

Every model call passes through a single choke point with schema validation, caching, per-tenant rate limits and spend caps. There is no second path to a provider.

Media behind signed links

Recordings sit in private storage reached only through short-lived signed URLs, with lifecycle rules that expire media on a schedule rather than keeping it indefinitely.

And plainly. A video round needs a working camera, a microphone and a connection — which is precisely why the system check runs first, and why the Voice and Chat rounds exist for candidates and roles where a camera is the wrong ask. We publish no accuracy figure for the scoring, because the honest measure is how often a human changed a score in your own pipeline — a number from your data rather than a claim about ours. Sentiment and body-language analysis is an input, not a verdict, and should never on its own be the reason somebody is passed over.

Frequently asked questions

What does a candidate need to take a video interview, and do they have to install an app?+
Nothing to install: the candidate does all of it in a browser. They need a working camera, microphone and connection, and before recording anything they run a short check on all three, with identity and document collection handled in chat first, so nobody loses a round to a broken headset and you only review people who were validated and ready.
How are the answers in a video interview scored?+
Every answer is scored against your own rubric by a model tier reserved for scoring, and the output is validated against a fixed schema rather than accepted as free text. Each score links back to the transcript excerpt and the moment of video it came from, so a rating can always be traced to something the candidate actually said.
Does the video interview analyse body language, and can that decide the outcome on its own?+
It reads emotional tone and confidence (posture, eye contact, gestures and composure) and turns them into structured, reviewable signal. That signal is presented as one input among several rather than a verdict, and a human still makes every call.
Can I run a live two-way interview or an avatar-led interview as well as the recorded round?+
Yes. Besides the recorded async round there is an avatar assessor that runs the identical script for every candidate, and a two-way live round that puts your own team in a real conversation. Both are scored against the same rubric as the async round, so a live score and a recorded score sit on the same axis.
Where are candidate video recordings stored, and are they kept indefinitely?+
Recordings sit in private storage that is reached only through short-lived signed URLs. Lifecycle rules expire the media on a schedule rather than keeping it indefinitely.
When is a video interview the wrong choice, and how accurate is the scoring?+
For candidates and roles where a camera is the wrong ask, TalbotIQ's Voice and Chat rounds exist instead of the video round. TalbotIQ publishes no accuracy figure for the scoring; the measure it points to is how often a human changed a score in your own pipeline.
